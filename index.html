NIST NICE Framework course

Learn the common language NIST uses to describe cybersecurity work and the capabilities people need to perform it. The framework helps connect learning, hiring, career development, and real team responsibilities.

01 Module 1

Why NICE exists

The Workforce Framework for Cybersecurity, NIST SP 800-181 Rev. 1, gives employers, learners, educators, and training providers a shared vocabulary for cybersecurity work. It describes work and capabilities; it does not prescribe one job title, org chart, or career path.

02 Module 2

Read the building blocks

Task, Knowledge, and Skill (TKS) statements are the foundation. Tasks describe work to be done. Knowledge and Skills describe what a person needs to know or be able to do. Work Roles group responsibility, while Competency Areas group related capabilities in a domain.

03 Module 3

Work roles are not job titles

A Work Role is a grouping of work for which an individual or team is responsible or accountable. A single job can combine several Work Roles, and a team can distribute one role's tasks across people with complementary experience.

04 Module 4

Recognize the role categories

The current component set organizes work across five broad categories: Oversight & Governance, Design & Development, Implementation & Operation, Protection & Defense, and Investigation. Component releases change over time, so use NIST's Current Versions page when exact labels or mappings matter.

05 Module 5

Use NICE in a learning plan

Start with a target Work Role or Competency Area, inspect its TKS statements, and turn the gaps into practice: a lab, a documented investigation, a system-hardening task, or a communication exercise. Keep evidence of what you can do, not only what you have read.

06 Module 6

Apply it to a cyber-physical team

In an energy-sector exercise, inventorying services, securing an industrial system, investigating an alert, and briefing leadership are different kinds of work. NICE helps a team describe those responsibilities precisely, identify missing capabilities, and plan cross-functional practice.

SOC 2 foundations course

A practical introduction to how service organizations define scope, map controls, prepare evidence, and understand SOC 2 reporting. Use this as a study guide, not as audit, legal, or compliance advice.

01 Module 1

Start with the frame

Learn what a SOC 2 engagement examines: a service organization’s system description and controls relevant to its services.

02 Module 2

Know the criteria

Study the Trust Services Criteria: security, availability, processing integrity, confidentiality, and privacy.

03 Module 3

Define the system

Practice scoping the services, people, processes, technology, data flows, vendors, and boundaries that support the service.

04 Module 4

Design the controls

Connect risks to control activities such as access reviews, change management, incident response, risk assessment, and vendor oversight.

05 Module 5

Build the evidence trail

Organize evidence by control, owner, period, system, and exception. Record gaps and remediation instead of hiding them.

06 Module 6

Read the report

Understand the difference between Type 1 reporting at a point in time and Type 2 reporting that also evaluates operating effectiveness over a period.
